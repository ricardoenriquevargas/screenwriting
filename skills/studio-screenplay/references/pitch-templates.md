# Pitch Package Templates

Read this when the user wants a logline, synopsis, treatment, one-pager, pitch deck or query letter.

## Logline
Formula: **When [inciting incident], a [specific flawed protagonist] must [goal] before/or [stakes], but [central obstacle/irony].**
- 25–40 words. No character names except famous ones; use vivid descriptors.
- Include the irony or hook that makes it a movie.
- Offer three versions: one plot-forward, one character-forward, one tone-forward.

Example: *When her gym is about to be bulldozed, a washed-up female boxer who never won a title must train the street kid who robbed her to fight the champion who ended her career.*

## One-page synopsis
Paragraph 1: protagonist, world, flaw, inciting incident.
Paragraph 2: the journey, midpoint shift, escalating stakes.
Paragraph 3: all-is-lost, climax, resolution and change. **Reveal the ending** — buyers expect it.
Present tense, third person, no camera language.

## Treatment (3–10 pages)
Title, logline, then the story in prose with the main beats, key scenes and the ending. Include tone notes and two or three moments of dialogue at most. Readable in 15 minutes.

## One-pager (for emails and markets)
Title · format · genre · logline · 150-word synopsis · comparables · target audience · status (script ready, attachments, budget range) · contact.

## Pitch deck (10–15 slides)
1. Title + key image 2. Logline 3. Why now / why this story 4. Tone and comparables 5. World 6. Main characters (one slide each for 2–3 leads) 7. Story overview (beginning–middle–end) 8. Visual references (described; the user supplies images they own or license) 9. Audience and market 10. For series: season arc and future seasons 11. Team / why the creator 12. Status and ask (what you're looking for: producer, financing, director) 13. Contact.

## Query letter (≤250 words)
Subject: `Query: TITLE – Genre Feature/Pilot`
1. Personalized opening (why this producer/agent: a project of theirs).
2. Logline + genre + format + comps.
3. One short paragraph of story.
4. Writer bio — only relevant credits, awards, lived experience.
5. Ask to send the script; thanks; contact.
Never attach the script unrequested; many companies only accept submissions through agents or with a signed release.
