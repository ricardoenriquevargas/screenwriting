# Common Mistakes That Get Scripts Rejected

Read this when reviewing pages or before delivering a draft. Run through the list and fix what applies.

## First 10 pages
- Opening with a character waking up, a dream sequence or an alarm clock.
- No clear protagonist by page 5; no hint of conflict by page 10.
- Exposition dumps: characters telling each other what they both know ("As you know, Mom…").
- Too many characters introduced at once.

## Writing style
- Dense action blocks (more than 4 lines).
- Unfilmables: thoughts, backstory, feelings the camera can't see ("She remembers her father").
- Directing on the page: excessive camera angles, "we see", "CLOSE ON".
- Passive voice and "-ing" constructions ("She is walking") instead of strong verbs ("She strides").
- Overusing parentheticals and CAPS.

## Dialogue
- On-the-nose dialogue: characters stating exactly what they feel.
- Every character sounds like the writer.
- Long monologues without a turn or reveal.
- Phonetic accents spelled out; use word choice and rhythm instead.

## Story
- Passive protagonist; things happen to them instead of choices driving the plot.
- Low or unclear stakes.
- Coincidences that solve problems (coincidences may create problems, never solve them).
- Climax resolved by someone other than the protagonist.
- Subplots that don't connect to the theme or the main plot.

## Presentation
- Wrong length (feature over 120 or under 85 pages without reason).
- Typos and format errors on page 1.
- Scene numbers, CONTINUED headers or "Copyright" notices on a spec script.
- A title page with draft dates or version numbers.
